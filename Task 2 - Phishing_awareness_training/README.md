# Phishing Awareness Training

This training provides an overview of phishing attacks and how to prevent them.

---

## Introduction

Phishing is a cyber attack that uses deceptive messages to trick victims into revealing sensitive data like login credentials, banking information, or personal details. [cite: 3, 4] These attacks often involve impersonating trusted sources through various communication channels. [cite: 4]

---

## Types of Phishing Attacks

* **Email Phishing:** Mass emails with fake links or attachments to steal credentials. [cite: 5]
   
* **Spear Phishing:** Personalized attacks targeting specific individuals. [cite: 6]
   
* **Whaling:** Attacks on high-profile individuals like executives. [cite: 7]
   
* **Smishing:** Phishing via SMS. [cite: 8]
   
* **Vishing:** Voice-based phishing. [cite: 9]
   
* **Clone Phishing:** Replicating legitimate emails with malicious links. [cite: 10]

---

## Recognizing Phishing Emails

* **Check Sender’s Email:** Look for misspellings or unfamiliar domains. [cite: 11]
   
* **Spot Errors:** Watch for poor grammar and spelling. [cite: 12]
   
* **Hover Over Links:** Verify URLs before clicking. [cite: 13]
   
* **Beware of Urgency:** Scammers create panic. [cite: 14]
   
* **Fake Attachments:** Avoid opening suspicious files. [cite: 15]

---

## Phishing Websites & Red Flags

* **Check for HTTPS & Padlock:** Secure sites use HTTPS, but it's not a guarantee of safety. [cite: 16]
   
* **Verify the Domain:** Look for misspellings in the URL. [cite: 17]
   
* **Beware of Pop-ups:** Fake login prompts can steal credentials. [cite: 18]
   
* **Avoid Unknown Downloads:** Malicious files can install malware. [cite: 19]

---

## Social Engineering Tactics

* **Pretexting:** Attackers impersonate trusted figures to manipulate victims. [cite: 20, 21]
   
* **Baiting:** Fraudsters use free offers or fake downloads to trick victims. [cite: 21, 22]
   
* **Quid Pro Quo:** Scammers offer benefits in exchange for credentials or access. [cite: 22]

---

## Best Practices to Stay Safe

* **MFA:** Enable Multi-Factor Authentication. [cite: 23]
   
* **Updates:** Regularly update software and patches. [cite: 23]
   
* **Passwords:** Use a password manager and strong, unique passwords. [cite: 24]
   
* **Reporting:** Report suspicious emails immediately. [cite: 24]
   
* **Verify Links:** Avoid clicking on unknown links. [cite: 25]
   
* **Secure Networks:** Use private Wi-Fi. [cite: 25, 26]
   
* **Data Backup:** Regularly back up data. [cite: 26, 27]

---

## What to Do If You Get Phished

* **Change Passwords:** Secure compromised accounts. [cite: 27, 28]
   
* **Report Incident:** Notify IT/Security teams. [cite: 28]
   
* **Monitor Accounts:** Check for unauthorized activity. [cite: 28]
   
* **Educate Others:** Share your experience. [cite: 28]
   
* **Isolate Devices:** Disconnect affected devices. [cite: 29]
   
* **Review Transactions:** Check for fraud. [cite: 29]
   
* **Document Everything:** Keep records of the incident. [cite: 30]
   
* **Consider Credit Freeze:** If sensitive data was exposed. [cite: 30]

---

## Conclusion

Phishing awareness training is essential for identifying phishing attacks and implementing prevention measures. [cite: 1, 2] By recognizing red flags, using security tools, and promoting a reporting culture, organizations can reduce the risk of phishing attacks. [cite: 2]
